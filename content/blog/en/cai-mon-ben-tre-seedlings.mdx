When people mention **Cai Mon, Ben Tre seedlings**, growers across Vietnam think of a region with a long tradition of seedlings, fruit trees and ornamental plants.

So what makes Cai Mon seedlings special, and how should you choose one? Let's take a look.

## Cai Mon – a region built on seedlings

![Cai Mon, Ben Tre seedling nursery with guava and durian](/images/about/story.webp)

Cai Mon lies in the Cho Lach area of Ben Tre, where natural conditions favour fruit trees and ornamental plants. The seedling trade here goes back a long way, along with propagation techniques such as **air layering and grafting**.

The Cai Mon – Vinh Thanh area is home to many family-run nurseries. Thanks to know-how passed down over generations, buyers can find many varieties and sizes in one place.

## Common seedlings

### Fruit trees

![Guava seedlings at a Cai Mon nursery](https://res.cloudinary.com/dsnlhndu/image/upload/v1789652056/oi-le-dai-loan.webp)

Guava, mango, jackfruit, durian, star apple, pomelo, orange, mandarin, rambutan, mangosteen, custard apple, avocado and more.

### Coconut

![Sprouted coconut seedlings at a Ben Tre nursery](/images/blog/cai-mon/coconut-seedlings.webp)

Ben Tre is famous for coconuts, so varieties such as **green Siamese, dwarf Siamese, red Siamese, pandan and macapuno coconut** are also widely produced, at several growth stages.

## Grafted or air-layered?

![Grafted seedling with a firm, healed graft union](/images/blog/cai-mon/grafted-seedling.webp)

- **Grafted:** the desired variety is joined onto a suitable rootstock and keeps the traits of the mother tree. When buying, check that the graft union is closed and firm.
- **Air-layered:** propagated from a branch of the mother tree. A good one has strong roots, balanced branches and no pests.

Not every variety suits the same method, so choose based on the plant and what you are growing it for.

## How to choose a healthy seedling

A big seedling isn't necessarily a good one. When buying, check:

1. **Trunk:** sturdy, not crooked or damaged.
2. **Leaves:** few disease spots, no caterpillar damage or unusual yellowing.
3. **Roots:** evenly developed, with an intact soil ball.
4. **Graft union:** closed, firm and free of cracks.
5. **Clear origin:** ask about the variety, propagation method, mother tree, age and size, and aftercare.

## What to keep in mind when buying

![Seedlings carefully packed before shipping to customers](/images/about/plants.webp)

Be clear about why you are planting. For a few trees at home, prioritise healthy, easy-care plants that fit the space. For a larger orchard, also consider soil, climate, spacing, time to first fruit, plant size and shipping.

Don't choose on price alone. A cheap, poor-quality seedling can cost you a lot of time and money later.
