Have a small garden and want to plant a few **fruit trees** for shade and clean, home-grown fruit for the family? You don't need anything exotic. A few familiar trees that suit your climate and space can already make a lovely corner of the garden.

Here are 3 fruit trees worth considering if you're just getting started.

## 1. Guava – easy to grow and quick to fruit

![A cluster of green guavas on a backyard tree](/images/blog/fruit-trees-home-garden/red-guava.webp)

Among fruit trees, guava is probably one of the most familiar choices for Vietnamese families.

A guava tree doesn't need a lot of land, and it can be pruned into a canopy that fits a home garden. When it grows well, its lush green branches hung with fruit are a pleasure to look at.

Beyond the familiar varieties, there are now many newer ones with different colours and flavours, such as **Red Lava guava** or Thai red guavas, for anyone who wants their garden to stand out a little.

If this is your first fruit tree, guava is an easy place to start.

## 2. Star apple – a touch of the countryside in your garden

![A cluster of golden star apples on the tree, leaves with bronze undersides](/images/blog/fruit-trees-home-garden/golden-star-apple.webp)

Star apple (vú sữa) has a character all its own in southern Vietnamese gardens. The tree has a handsome canopy and green leaves, and once mature it gives good shade.

And picking a ripe star apple from your own garden feels very different from buying fruit at the market.

There are many star apple varieties too. Alongside the familiar ones, varieties like the **golden-fleshed hoàng kim star apple** are drawing interest for their unusual colour and shape.

If your garden has the space and you want a tree that gives both fruit and shade, star apple is well worth considering.

## 3. Custard apple – for those who love sweet fruit

![Thai custard apples on a backyard tree, with a bamboo fence behind](/images/blog/fruit-trees-home-garden/thai-custard-apple.webp)

Custard apple (na) is a family favourite for its fragrant, sweet fruit and soft flesh.

There's more than the traditional variety. In recent years, **Thai custard apple** has become popular for its large, good-looking fruit.

When planting custard apple, the key is a spot with plenty of light and well-drained soil. Starting with a healthy seedling also gives the tree a better chance once it goes into the ground.

## Choose fruit trees to suit your garden

No single fruit tree suits every garden.

If your yard is small, favour trees whose size is easy to manage, like guava. With more space, star apple or custard apple are interesting options.

Also think about how much light you get, how well your soil drains and how much time you can spend on care.

Most importantly, **don't choose a tree just because it looks good in someone else's garden**. Choose varieties that suit your own.

## Even a small garden can have fruit trees

Growing fruit trees doesn't have to start with a big garden.

A guava in the corner of the yard, a star apple for shade or a custard apple for sweet seasons can all make your home feel warmer.

If you're looking for **fruit trees to grow at home**, start with a few varieties that suit your conditions. Once you've gained some experience, you can keep adding new ones to the garden.
